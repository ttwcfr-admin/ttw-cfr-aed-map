      Method and assumptions
      • Defibrillators: the latest national register extract (The Circuit). Units up to 3 km outside the area are kept because they cover border streets.
      • Population: Census 2021 usual residents and residents aged 65+ for every Output Area, spread across each area's residential postcodes so people sit where homes are, then mapped on a 100 m grid. Only postcodes in TN1–TN4 and TN9–TN12 are counted.
      • Area boundary: postcode district outlines are drawn from ONS postcode centroids, so edges are approximate.
      • Retrieval distance: Resuscitation Council UK advises an AED no more than a 2-minute brisk walk away (about 100 m); most ambulance services use at least 200 m when directing callers to one. Retrieval is modelled as halving with every 100 m walked, with straight-line distance stretched by 25% for real routes.
      • Availability: public 24/7 units count in full, public limited-hours units at 35%, restricted units not at all.
      • Risk weighting: residents aged 65+ count 3.5×, and a deprivation uplift of up to 1.5× comes from the 2025 Index of Multiple Deprivation.

      Limits: the model uses where people live, so daytime footfall (town centres, stations, sports grounds) is not captured. Check every suggested spot on the ground for a safe, lit, accessible wall and a power supply.

      Sources: The Circuit national defibrillator network; ONS Census 2021 via Nomis; ONS Postcode Directory and Output Area boundaries; OS Open Roads (contains OS data © Crown copyright and database right 2026); © OpenStreetMap contributors; Deakin et al., Heart 2018;104:1339–43.
